# Katy Technologies Brand System Spec

Date: 2026-05-24

Purpose: this is the agent-readable brand system source of truth for Katy Technologies. Use this file when creating websites, PDFs, social assets, decks, proposals, UI components, or future brand materials. This is not a spec for a particular brand guide PDF; it is the durable brand specification.

## 1. Brand Definition

Katy Technologies is a consulting company that builds technological solutions to automate workflows.

Canonical offering:

> We help enterprise teams automate manual workflows with practical AI and custom software.

Primary audience:

- Enterprise executives.
- Operational leaders responsible for internal systems, risk, and throughput.
- Buyers who know manual processes are slowing teams down and who are cautious about AI automation.

Desired response:

- Reassured.
- Understood.
- Confident that automation can be practical, governed, and human.

Brand tension:

- Efficient yet human.
- Technically precise yet warm.
- Automation-forward without AI hype.
- Enterprise-credible without generic consulting language.

## 2. Voice and Messaging

Voice:

- Warm.
- Advisory.
- Humanistic.
- Plainspoken.
- Specific.

Writing principles:

- Start with the human operational problem.
- Describe technology as a practical support system, not the hero.
- Prefer concrete workflow language over abstraction.
- Make risk and trust visible.
- Sound like an experienced advisor talking through the problem with the client.

Primary CTA:

> Talk through a bottleneck

Approved CTA variants:

- Map a workflow.
- Find the slow handoff.
- Talk with us.

Approved headline examples:

- Automate the work that slows your team down.
- Start with the bottleneck, not the tool.
- Automation should make work easier to trust, not harder to explain.

Approved service copy:

> We map the way work actually moves through your organization, then build the software and AI support that removes repeat manual steps without creating new risk.

Avoid:

- Digital transformation.
- Cutting-edge.
- Innovation.
- Synergy.
- Leverage.
- Seamless.
- Disrupt.
- Future-proof.
- Revolutionary.
- Unlock your potential.
- AI-powered everything.

Rewrite jargon into human operational language. For example, rewrite "cutting-edge AI solutions for digital transformation" as "practical AI support for workflows that still depend on manual review."

Primary logo behavior:

- Keep the original mark: white `K` and arrow, translucent muted blue-gray `T`, deep navy field when shown as official primary identity.
- Do not apply knockout-under-`T` to the primary full-color logo.

Supporting logo behavior:

- Use the one-ink navy knockout variant on light backgrounds when the `T` must visibly sit on top.
- Use the dark-surface white/slate variant on dark navy, blueprint, grain, or grid backgrounds.
- Use one-color variants only for reproduction constraints.
- Do not define same-color translucent `T` over same-color opaque `K` as a standard logo behavior; the overlap becomes visually invisible.

Logo over diagrams and textures:

- Website: direct overlay is allowed only when the diagram is decorative and quiet.
- Presentations and slide decks: watermark-style logo is allowed as a background texture.
- Background texture marks must use the K/arrow cutout under the `T`.
- Functional diagrams must not be obscured by the logo.

Clearspace:

- Minimum clearspace is 1x the approximate width of the `T` vertical stem.
- Use 1.5x clearspace near grids, diagrams, grain, or other active textures.

Minimum sizes:

- Web navigation: at least 32px tall.
- Print: at least 0.45 inches tall unless using a simplified one-color export.
- Watermark: may be oversized and cropped, but never as the only identifying logo.

## 4. Color System

Palette rule:

- Stay strictly within logo colors plus neutrals.
- Tints, shades, and opacity variations are allowed.
- Do not introduce new accent hues.

Core colors:

- Deep Navy: `#071A33`
- Dark Blueprint Navy: `#06192D`
- Mid Blueprint Navy: `#0A213A`
- Deep Slate: `#263D54`
- Soft Slate: `#536B80`
- Muted Blue-Gray: `#7F95AA`
- White: `#FFFFFF`
- Warm Paper: `#F8F7F2`

Allowed contrast methods:

- Navy shades.
- Blue-gray tints.
- White and warm paper neutrals.
- Opacity steps.
- Line style, marker shape, labels, and pattern.

Do not use:

- Green success.
- Red error.
- Orange/yellow warning.
- Purple or cyan technology gradients.
- Rainbow chart palettes.

Recommended opacity ladder:

- 100%.
- 86%.
- 72%.
- 58%.
- 42%.
- 24%.
- 12%.

## 5. Gradients, Grain, and Grid

Gradient direction:

- Blueprint Fade.
- Dark, technical, and blueprint-like.
- Directional linear gradients, not radial glows.
- A gradient may follow a Bezier-like path with a small number of control points using SVG shapes, masks, or layered directional gradients.

Do not use:

- Radial gradients.
- Neon glows.
- Multicolor palettes outside the logo colors.
- Purple/blue AI SaaS gradients.

Grain:

- Fine and dense.
- Small speckles.
- Photo-grain feel.
- Must not reduce text contrast.
- Must not muddy brand colors.
- Should reduce banding and create atmosphere.

Grid:

- Use rarely.
- Strongest in diagrams and selected section openers.
- Content-area grid lines should generally stay below 12% opacity.
- Stronger grids are allowed in technical diagrams or cover/hero atmospheres.

## 6. Typography

Approved type pairing:

- Work Sans.
- Literata.

Work Sans usage:

- All headers and headings.
- UI labels.
- Captions.
- Navigation.
- Buttons.
- Technical metadata.

Literata usage:

- Body copy.
- Explanatory prose.
- Advisory text.
- Long-form brand-guide content.
- Editorial warmth.

Do not use:

- Inter as primary brand type.
- Montserrat as final heading type.
- Generic system font as a visible brand choice.
- Handwriting fonts as permanent brand typography.

Handwritten layer:

- Agents must not generate faux handwritten annotations, simulated handwriting, sketch-like SVG doodles, or handwriting-font substitutes.
- Use explicit image placeholders until Katy Technologies provides real handwritten annotation assets.
- Each placeholder must flag that the user needs to provide the asset, for example: `[USER-ASSET NEEDED: handwritten annotation marking the manual review bottleneck]`.
- Use only user-provided real handwritten image assets when replacing placeholders.
- Do not use handwriting fonts as the final system.
- Handwritten elements should be annotations on precise diagrams, not generic doodles.

## 7. Diagram and Icon Language

Approved direction:

- Precise system diagrams with handwritten annotations.

Diagram elements:

- Process nodes.
- Handoff lines.
- Decision branches.
- System boundaries.
- Inputs and outputs.
- Risk markers.
- Annotation callouts.
- Measurement ticks.
- Subtle grid references.

Handwritten annotations:

- Should feel human and advisory.
- Should identify specific bottlenecks, risks, or decisions.
- Should remain secondary to the precise system diagram.

Iconography:

- Use diagrammatic marks and line symbols.
- Prefer simple line symbols over filled icon sets.
- Use consistent line weight.
- Avoid stock icon libraries that feel generic.

Do not use:

- Photography.
- Cartoon illustrations.
- Decorative doodles without operational meaning.
- Generic SaaS icon packs.

## 8. Layout and Components

Overall visual feel:

- Technical operating system.
- Sharp.
- Precise.
- Warmed by typography and annotations, not by soft rounded shapes.

Shape language:

- Sharp and rectilinear.
- Avoid visibly nested parent/child card stacks.
- Place modules side by side so edges are implied by spacing, alignment, faint rules, and tonal shifts.

Borders:

- Use very faint hairlines.
- Hairlines should support structure without boxing everything in.
- Avoid heavy borders and thick dividers except for deliberate technical examples.

Component examples should follow these rules:

- Hero: atmospheric blueprint gradient, clear human headline, CTA "Talk through a bottleneck."
- Navigation: restrained, sharp, quiet, with logo clearspace.
- Buttons: precise, direct, no hype.
- Cards/modules: adjacent planes, not nested cards.
- Forms: advisory and low-friction.
- Diagrams: precise base with handwritten annotations.
- Charts/tables: logo colors, tints, shades, opacity; no new hues.
- Social/OG: grainy blueprint atmosphere, short humanistic headline, no photos.

## 9. Accessibility and Legibility

Core rule:

- Atmosphere should never make the work harder to understand.

Text contrast:

- WCAG AA minimum 4.5:1 for normal text.
- WCAG AA minimum 3:1 for large display text.

Logo:

- Primary logo should sit on navy or sufficiently dark blueprint gradient.
- Use protected spacing when grids or diagrams become visually active.
- Use approved supporting logo assets for light/dark surfaces.

Grid:

- Below body copy, grid lines should generally remain below 12% opacity.
- Stronger grids are for diagrams, technical examples, cover art, or section openers.

Grain:

- Fine and dense.
- Must not lower contrast below accessibility thresholds.

Diagrams:

- Functional diagrams must prioritize labels and paths.
- Decorative diagrams may sit behind logos only when they do not cross core logo shapes.
- Handwritten annotations must be readable and purposeful.

Motion:

- Motion should clarify flow.
- Avoid continuous animation near body copy.
- Respect reduced-motion preferences.

## 10. Brand Applications

PDFs:

- Compact and manual-like interiors.
- Full-page atmospheric cover.
- US Letter portrait when producing the brand guide.

Websites:

- Use brand system rules, but define website structure in a separate website overhaul spec.
- Website examples inside brand materials should be components, not final IA decisions.

Presentations:

- Watermark logo textures are approved.
- Use K cutout under `T` for watermark marks.
- Do not rely on watermark as the only identifying logo.

Social/OG:

- No photography.
- Use grain, grid, diagram language, and concise humanistic messaging.
