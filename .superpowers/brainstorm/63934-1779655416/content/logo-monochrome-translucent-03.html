Monochrome T-On-Top Treatments

A same-color translucent T over an opaque same-color K is mathematically invisible at overlaps. These options make the T visibly topmost while staying monochrome/tonal.

T above · tonal monochrome
A · Tonal Navy/Slate
K remains opaque navy; T sits on top as a lighter slate tone. This is the most legible “monochrome family” treatment.
K masked below · T above
B · Knockout Under T
The K is reduced beneath the T, then the translucent T sits above. This makes the top layer visible in true one-ink usage.
T above · tonal monochrome
C · White/Slate On Navy
Closest to the primary mark while still behaving as a restrained tonal system on dark surfaces.
T above · tonal monochrome
D · Navy On Slate
Works for large diagram/panel use, but the T will only read clearly where it extends beyond the K unless knockout is used.
Recommendation for the guide: do not define “same color, translucent T over opaque K” as a standard logo because the overlap cannot be seen. Use either a tonal T or a knockout-under-T export when top-layer visibility matters.
